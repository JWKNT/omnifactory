# An independent supply for the blast furnace

The fourth aluminium charge exposed the limit of the workshop circuit. The furnace draws 120 EU/t through two LV hatches. A full four-amp LV converter can deliver 128 EU/t, so other workshop machines can slow the blast even when that converter has stored energy.

The surveyed route starts at the Energetic Alloy RF conduit at (8,82,7). Place a Basic Capacitor Bank on its west face at (7,82,7), then run four Conductive Iron Energy Conduits west to (3,82,7). These RF conduit tiers do not connect directly in this instance. The bank joins the two networks and handles 1,000 RF/t. The cheaper conduit handles 512 RF/t, sufficient for four LV amps. A direct energetic conduit extension would also work but costs more energetic alloy. The bank earns quest 33 and puts the four spare Basic RF Capacitors to work.

Use a new four-amp LV CEF at (3,81,7), facing down into the existing lossless fourfold cable to both hatches. The existing workshop trunk stays on its original converter. Build the CEF, bank and conduit first. The order uses eight low-tier conduits including the four inside the CEF. The assembler yields four per operation, compared with three at the bench.

The feeder was surveyed and queued while the 40-ingot batch was running. scripts/ebf_feeder.py checks the controller and input bus before it removes the old upper cable. It verifies the replacement converter faces down before adding its RF connection. Commissioning requires charge at the new converter and a subsequent blast under workshop load.

The workshop crafted the Basic Capacitor Bank. The book paid one nickel for quest 33 and the separate four-amp CEF is still on order. The bank alone does not change the blast furnace power supply.

The bank is installed at (7,82,7). After a single diamond entered the Numismatic Dynamo, the bank rose from zero to 335,222 RF. Its west output is enabled. The dedicated CEF and downstream conduit still need installation.

The deuterium electrolyzer needs (4,82,7), on the original four-conduit RF route. The bank is installed and all six positions on the revised route were air. Leave the west face at (6,82,7), rise to (6,83,7), go west at y83 to x3, then drop to (3,82,7) above the CEF. This costs two extra Conductive Iron Energy Conduits. The installer now budgets all six automatically. It also clears the water source at (5,82,7) and the planned compressor interface one block south.

During the MV assembler build the EBF accepted 21 aluminium dust. It produced ingots while the controller intermittently showed insufficient energy at 120 EU/t. The lapidary dynamo had three queued diamonds and the two LV hatches had 0 and 96 EU at the sample. The stores already held nine Conductive Iron Energy Conduits and both fourfold wires required by the new CEF. The assembler order now plans the CEF and its remaining conduit batch and checks for an idle furnace before the changeover.

The terminal built the four-amp LV CEF during the assembler order and the existing bank reached 1,000,000 RF with its west output enabled. Five external conduits remain in stock after the four used inside the CEF. Six are required for installation. A binder batch is ready and the remaining conduit order can also run alongside the MV electrolyzer. The original blast feed is still in service.
