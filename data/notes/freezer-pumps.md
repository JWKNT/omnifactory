# Hardware for the Vacuum Freezer controller

Quest 150 pays a quarter for the controller. It takes three HV pumps, three EV circuits, one frost-proof casing and two single gold cables. The pumps add another nine gold cables, including the two on each HV motor. Check loose pumps and motors before ordering eleven insulated gold cables. Use the established selector-24 wrapping line under the LV assembler. Three motors also need twelve fourfold copper wires, six stainless rods and three magnetic steel rods. The wiremill and insulated single copper cable already have ME patterns.

There are six pump recipes. Each pump consumes one steel rotor, one screw, one medium stainless pipe, one HV motor and one gold cable. The bench adds two rings. The 480 EU/t assembler instead takes 144 mB ordinary rubber, 108 mB styrene-butadiene rubber or 72 mB silicone rubber. The HV assembler now holds solder for circuits. A ring die in the installed MV extruder makes four ordinary rubber rings from one rubber bar in ten ticks. Two bars supply all three bench pumps and two spare rings. The same die makes four steel rings per steel ingot.

The three steel rotors can also be cast. The rotor mold is already on the shelf, but melting steel costs 128 EU/t, above the installed LV extractors. The LV assembler makes each rotor from four plates, one steel ring and 32 mB solder. An ordinary grid uses the same metal parts plus a screw per rotor. For three stainless pipes the MV extruder uses nine ingots and the normal pipe die. The existing large pipe die has a different shape. The bench turns six plates into two pipes, so two bench batches cost twelve ingots and leave a spare pipe. Count the pipe die's cost across later chemical builds. Keep the loose stainless plates for the electronics order until the 36-dust stainless charge is funded.

The first power and port layout is in vacuum-freezer.md. Form the multiblock, retrieve the nine banked hot ingots and verify a finished cold Kanthal ingot before diverting further hot metal into the input bus.

The ordinary mill drew eighteen gold wires from nine ingots. Seventeen passed through the selector-24 LV wrapping recipe and reached ME. Eleven are budgeted across the controller and three complete pumps. Six are for the second furnace feeder. The terminal also finished twelve quadruple copper wires for the motors. The polarizer supplied all three magnetic steel rods. For rotors, the existing HV assembler can run the 32 mB solder recipe and return them through its ME interface. Reserve the controller's 216 mB of solder first.

At 36h24m ME held all three finished HV motors. Their combined bill was twelve quadruple copper wires, six insulated gold cables, six stainless rods and three magnetic steel rods. Resume at the three rotors, three screws, six rubber rings and three medium stainless pipes. Three more insulated gold cables join those parts and the motors in the pump recipes.

The water saw cut eighteen plates from two steel blocks. Eight plates became two reusable extruder dies for rings and normal pipes. The first steel ingot through the ring die made four rings. ME received the first two soldered steel rotors, and the compressor accepted the last two plates' worth of steel for the third. Two rubber sheets went through the macerator and their pulp went to the electric furnace. At the water saw one steel rod gives four bolts, and the lathe makes one screw per bolt. Two rods from a single ingot fund eight screws.

The final pump order is complete. The ring die produced eight rubber rings, the normal pipe die produced three medium stainless pipes, and the gear die is back in the MV extruder. Two rods yielded eight bolts in the water saw and eight screws in the lathe. The terminal made the three pumps, leaving two rubber rings and five screws from those batches. The pumps are now in the formed Vacuum Freezer.
