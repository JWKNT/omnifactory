# Keep the original blast furnace working through titanium

The old furnace already has independent power and fluid hatches. It has been alternating between aluminium for hulls, vibrant alloy for electronics, and stainless steel. A second furnace would let those orders continue during the much longer titanium and later hot-metal runs. Both plans need sixteen new Kanthal coils. The incremental expense of keeping the old furnace is the second casing shell, buses, hatches, controller and connections.

The north site at x11–13, z−4 through −2, y80–83 is clear. Six of the nine foundation blocks are already cobblestone. Extend the floor east from x12 with the builder’s wand and leave a service aisle around the outside. An upward HV hatch at (11,83,-2) can connect to the double gold feeder at (11,82,2) with six single gold cables by taking the north face first to (11,82,1), rising twice there and crossing north at y84. I checked every adjacent tile. The feed passes a furnace, an ME cable and the existing HV reactor. It leaves the planned fluid terminal east of (10,83,2) accessible. This route also leaves the east MV expansion clear.

Live JEI gives three Heat Proof Machine Casings for six Invar plates and one Invar frame. A shell with one controller, two item buses, two fluid hatches and one energy hatch needs twelve casing blocks plus a thirteenth in the controller. Five recipes make fifteen. The controller also takes three ordinary furnaces, three basic-grade circuits and two single tin cables. Count existing spares before crafting. The HV input hatch, Kanthal, and hot-ingot transfer still need funding. The old westward HV cable plan required fourteen new cables.

At four RF per EU, a 480 EU/t titanium blast, 120 EU/t old furnace recipe, and 120 EU/t freezer draw 2,880 RF/t together before losses and other machinery. The hardened numismatic dynamo is rated at 3,750 RF/t. Watch its actual sustained fuel and the CEF buffers during simultaneous jobs.
