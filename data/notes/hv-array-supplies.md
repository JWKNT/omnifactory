# Materials for the three LV Electronic Processor Arrays

The HV converter takes one Advanced circuit. The two arms for the HV assembler take one each. Use the LV Electronic Processor Array for these three slots. The two Refined Processor Mainframes stay earmarked for the assembler. The oven already holds four buckets of phenol, enough to coat forty blanks at 100 mB per board.

From empty stock, the three arrays take twelve Electronic Processors and 36 Basic Electronic Circuits. Across those 51 assembly steps, the leaded recipes use 198 resistors, 78 capacitors and twelve transistors. The wire demands are 192 fine copper wires and 48 fine electrum wires. Reserve six Integrated Circuits and six aluminium plates for the last three assembly steps. There are four finished Phenolic Circuit Boards and an Integrated Circuit Wafer already on the shelf. The cutter can turn that wafer into eight Integrated Circuits with 90 mB water.

The bench makes three plain Coated Circuit Boards from two rubber and three wooden slabs. Four mature rubber trees stand east of the workshop. Harvest and replant them to supply rubber and logs for blanks while the mainframe assembler runs. A second rubber source is the Slime model, whose pristine yields 32 slimeballs. Use the shared MV reactor for ten blanks at a time when its clathrate inputs are clear. Ten blanks empty one bucket of phenol, which leaves room to run clathrate again between batches.

The MV mainframe order also requires one processor made with two ordinary resistors, capacitors and transistors. The bench transistor recipe makes four from four aluminium foils, two rubber sheets and three fine silver wires. One aluminium plate in the cluster mill supplies all four foils. Save the two surplus transistors for the LV arrays. The alternative assembler recipe yields eight, but consumes 144 mB polyethylene, six tin foil and a silicon plate. The mainframe assembler has already switched over to solder.

The grove harvest delivered thirty logs and all four plots were replanted. The next survey counted fifty rubber. The engraved Integrated Circuit Wafer went into the cutter for eight chips. The first fine copper order uses the ME wiremill to draw sixteen singles from eight ingots, then the original LV wiremill to draw 64 fine wires. This leaves the ME wiremill available during the second step.

The first two mainframes are complete. Page 132 paid two OmniQuarters. The next three LV arrays have 42 blank boards available from fourteen bench crafts. The independent wafer cutter finished the eight Integrated Circuits. For the first sixty conventional resistors, the spare MV assembler can use twenty fine copper wires and five coal dust. The original LV assembler remains available for the soldered circuits. Two stored electrum ingots reduce the remaining 48-wire bill to four more ingots, made from two gold and two silver. Eight of the seventeen stored Gold Ore have entered the grinder, yielding two crushed ore per block. Hammer the sixteen crushed ore to impure dust and smelt for sixteen additional gold. This reserves nine ore for later.
