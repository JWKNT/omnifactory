# ME produces its own workshop interfaces

The third Molecular Assembler shelf at (12,82,9) advertises full ME Interfaces, MV Robot Arms, quadruple copper wire and HV Electric Motors. The arm pattern accepts a Refined Processor for its Good circuit. Its first arm went into the third shelf. Building more pattern capacity had previously meant fetching the arm ingredients and crafting each interface at the terminal.

The EV electrolyzer now has a full interface on its west side at (17,82,-9). Its actual encoded pattern asks for seven Tungstate Dust and promises one Tungsten Dust and two Lithium Dust. The hydrogen comes through the fluid bus and oxygen has a separate return. The first four-unit processing order is the commissioning test. Check run/native-tungsten-delivered before counting the order as proven.

The east furnace's cold tungsten and tungstensteel can use the same freezer return. The proposed input route leaves the existing Fluix cable at (18,81,-9), follows y81,z-10 east to x27, and drops an interface against the south side of the bottom input bus. Each tungstensteel order should promise two cold ingots per tungsten and steel pair. Keep the ore grinding and centrifuging stages running while the furnace heats.

The four-unit tungsten order finished with all four dust in storage. The electrolyzer took 896 ticks per dust at 1,920 EU/t and kept its hydrogen tank full. All ten Fluix cables along the east furnace aisle are in place. The furnace input interface is still pending.

The washer's Infinite Water Source now sits below it at (12,81,3). Its tank still read 64,000 mB. The silicone cable assembler has an ME interface on its west at (12,83,3). The first four-aluminium-cable order exposed an installation fault. Two bare wires went into the washer below the interface, while the other two became cable. Turning the full interface east with the Yeta Wrench and moving those two wires through the wrapper finished the old CPU job. A fresh unattended order is the proof needed here. The helper scripts/interface_direction.py finds a sequence of visible face clicks to turn a full interface even when the opposite face is obstructed.
