# A stack-fed inscriber for later ME hardware

The basic inscriber at (11,81,9) takes one piece at a time. Two interface builds, additional drives and crafting CPUs will repeat the same pressing and assembly operations. Page 140 is open and pays one nickel. Its Advanced Inscriber accepts stacks, locks presses and can be supplied by ME processing patterns. It needs an external output extractor for unattended delivery.

Live JEI gives one upgrade recipe. It consumes the existing Inscriber, two Engineering Processors, two hoppers and four ordinary iron ingots. Each hopper takes five iron plates and a chest at the bench, with a reusable wrench. The assembler can make it from five iron or wrought plates, a chest and a selector. The survey found one Engineering Processor and 180 diamonds. The second processor needs a printed diamond plate, printed silicon and one of the three basic-grade circuits. There is no direct Advanced Inscriber reward. Finish the current Logic Processors first. Preserve the press, then test the replacement in the same powered socket before spending another interface on it. Request a multi-item silicon print at that machine to verify the upgrade earns its place.

scripts/advanced_inscriber.py is queued after the network furnace's three terminal trials. It sources the whole upgrade bill before collecting the empty basic machine, opens page 140 in the book, and tests a two-plate batch. The basic inscriber is still installed.

At 31h46m the live Engineering Processor recipe showed three possible middle components. There was one finished Engineering Processor and no stocked qualifying circuit. The terminal held ten resistors, two ordinary capacitors, six phenolic boards and 114 copper ingots. The LV assembler had 72 mB solder. That is one Basic Electronic Circuit using one board, four resistors, two capacitors and four fine copper wires. The wiremill already has the copper patterns. The upgrade now checks these supplies when it needs its second processor. The four-output Refined Circuit recipe is attractive for the coming freezer batch, but the current stock has only two SMD capacitors and all eighteen CPUs have a job there. A finished circuit still takes priority.

The north furnace is commissioned. A first ordinary production order returned another eight Pulsating Dust while the inscriber order was active. For the second Engineering Processor I cut a diamond block on the MV saw and received nine plates. One went through the Engineering Press. The equivalent compressor recipe needs one diamond and 100 base ticks per plate. The saw uses one block and 9 mB water for nine plates in 192 base ticks. The remaining eight plates can serve later presses. A block of silicon similarly gives nine plates for 21 mB water. The workshop had 51 silicon ingots and the three processor types all use printed silicon.

At 32h15m I claimed page 140 and its nickel, installed the Advanced Inscriber in the old powered socket, and put two silicon plates into its middle slot together. I collected two Printed Silicon from that one load. It is ready for stack printing. A full ME processing line still needs output extraction.

The circuit decision changed at the bench. Making the furnace interface had left two ordinary resistors instead of ten. I had no spare red alloy cables or tubes for a Basic Circuit. The dry HV assembler returned twelve resistors on one ME order and the wiremill supplied four more fine copper wires. The LV assembler then used four resistors, four wires, the two stocked capacitors, one phenolic board and its last 72 mB of solder to make the Basic Electronic Circuit. That circuit went between the two prints and made the second Engineering Processor. Count affordable ME batches as well as finished parts when choosing a circuit. A separate iron block on the saw supplied nine of the ten plates for the upgrade's two hoppers.
