# Feeding the first aluminium batch

Quest 69 requires a GregTech aluminium ingot after the blast furnace quest. This instance's JEI shows a reliable LV source of aluminium dust in the Basic Electrolyzer. Seven clay dust take 400 ticks at 30 EU/t and yield two sodium, two silicon, one lithium and two aluminium dust. One aluminium dust in the EBF takes 400 ticks at 120 EU/t (48,000 EU per ingot). The installed CEF accepts a large charge before a run: 1.49 million EU was measured while the workshop was active. Verify the two LV energy hatches are being fed and see the first ingot through before building a larger power plant.

The aluminium ore in the camp and void stores is libvulpes:ore0@9. JEI lists furnace smelting into libvulpes:productingot@9 and no uses for that ingot. Reserve the ore until a real use is verified. Ordinary bauxite dust electrolysis also makes aluminium, but that particular recipe needs 60 EU/t and an MV electrolyzer. The clay route works in LV and supplies silicon for the circuit line. Check the live clay, electrolyzer and furnace recipes when ordering the machines.

Clay is exposed under the lake just west of the initial sugar cane plot. Shallow blocks around (-214,61,203) lead to a larger bed. Twenty-four clay balls came back; 21 were ground into clay dust with the iron mortar and recipe index 1. That makes three electrolyzer batches. An LV electrolyzer needs a hull, two basic circuits, four gold wires, one tin cable and one glass. The planner supports gregtech:machine@240. Quest 260 detects the machine and all the clay products, so collect every output from its separate slots.

The basic electrolyzer now runs at (4,81,6), west of the workshop power cable. The first three clay batches yielded six aluminium dust, six sodium, six silicon and three lithium. The output slots were collected, quest 260 paid a nickel, and the EBF's first two aluminium ingots completed quest 69. There is a second clay source already at the factory. Eight gray terracotta from Lost Cities floors gave 32 clay dust in the macerator. Twenty-eight went into the electrolyzer for another eight aluminium, bringing the combined furnace order to fourteen ingots. Reserve nine plates for the first MV hull. The casing costs eight; the hull recipe also needs one plate, two wrought plates and two single insulated copper cables.

The first MV hull has been crafted and quest 259 paid an omniquarter. The furnace finished all fourteen ingots. Nine went into plates for the hull. The other five were visible in the EBF output bus at the end of the run. The CEF held 1.37 million EU at that point. An isolated MV cable and CEF will be needed when the hull becomes a powered MV machine; the existing four-amp LV line still runs the single-block workshop.

The second 32-dust blast furnace charge is complete. The output bus delivered twelve, ten and ten aluminium ingots. Collecting during a long charge let the engraver start the Logic Press without waiting for all 32. The same clay electrolysis took the lithium dust reserve to 64. All 64 were fired into ingots and checked on quest 615, Element 003: Lithium. The book paid a quarter and the ingots returned to storage.

The third 32-ingot reserve charge has started. The LV electrolyzer brought the dust stock to 34. The furnace took 32 and its input bus held 31 while the first ingot was processing at 120 EU/t. The two spare dust can cover cobalt brass. Each ingot takes 400 ticks, so the nominal batch time is 10 min 40 s. The workshop can collect the first ingot early for the Drive's eighth aluminium plate. The batch runs alongside the other metal and circuit jobs, with a 10,000 mB oxygen target for plastic and annealing.

The third 32-dust charge is finished. The final pickup removed two aluminium ingots from the output bus and returned them to storage. The blast controller was idle and the input bus empty. The Drive order already has its eight aluminium plates, including the plates used in the finished MV sensor.

After the Drive went online, the stores held 31 aluminium ingots, two dust and 42 clay dust. Quest 625 pays an OmniQuarter for showing 64 aluminium ingots and returns the stack to the player. The next MV machines also need aluminium. A 40-ingot charge is queued alongside the LV smelter and brewery orders. Each of twenty clay batches yields two dust and keeps the two banked dust available. At 48,000 EU per ingot the blast charge costs 1.92 million EU. The workshop had fifty diamonds and the north MV CEF was full. The new aluminium-bounty request checks the stack before it is distributed to later hulls. Use the blast furnace for this ingot.

The fourth charge was submitted in full. The input bus showed 39 aluminium dust while the EBF processed the first at 120 EU/t. Two ingots were visible in the output bus while the new polymer smelter ran its first batch. Four collections of 2, 10, 17 and 11 ingots brought home all 40. The storage survey verified 71 aluminium ingots. Quest 625 registered when 64 were carried and paid one OmniQuarter. The furnace is clear.

The MV assembler order still requires aluminium for its robot arms, conveyors and hull. The survey during the Pattern Terminal build found fifteen ingots, two dust, 34 clay dust and 69 gray terracotta. The reserve order calls for 24 additional ingots. scripts/mv_aluminium.py can start the EBF at eight available dust while the electrolyzer supplies the rest. It keeps one metal dust, eight clay dust and sixteen terracotta in reserve. Starting the EBF in smaller charges lets the wiremill and lathe keep making motor parts alongside it.
