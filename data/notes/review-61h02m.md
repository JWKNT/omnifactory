# What stopped parallel production at 61 hours

In two played hours I loaded 166 ingredient stacks, up from 116 at the last review. The transfer log verified three ME orders by rising stock. It misses continuous transfers, so it cannot measure all machine output. I moved 192,000 mB oxygen in thirteen trips and 53,000 mB sulfuric acid in eleven. All three crafting CPUs were busy in 14 of 658 samples, and all three were idle in 370. The immediate investment is inputs and return paths. A fourth CPU only becomes useful more often once those three have work.

The EV reactor and electrolyzer can run together on the new four-amp supply. The electrolyzer returned all four buckets of radon to storage while the ore reactor worked through the second miner's cargo. Both titanium orders returned cold ingots through ME. The sulfuric gas reactor made four more batches after a single hydrogen charge. At the same time the EV reactor needed a player to drain acid, take out concentrate, refill its input with that same concentrate, add aluminium, and remove lead-zinc solution. Adding a faster reactor alone would leave every one of those trips.

Oxygen was the largest transport job. First finish the native return from the new electrolyzer and oxygen export to the acid reactor. The 56 tungstate dust already on hand can yield 32,000 mB oxygen alongside eight tungsten and sixteen lithium. The test is to see oxygen leave the electrolyzer and sulfuric acid appear in storage for at least two reactions while the player works elsewhere. The existing export bus below the raw gas reactor can take hydrogen on a second filter. A capacity card costs much less space than another cable branch. Page 730 also describes a stocked fluid interface and pump or regulator. For the next *new* bank of reactors, quote that layout first. The interface is five lapis plates, an MV pump, both cores and an MV hull, plus the cover at the consumer. Leave an accessible service face at every reactor.

The LV centrifuge has a new job. Its first 3,000 mB of indium by-product yielded three lead, three silver, three zinc, nine sulfur and 3,000 mB water. Give nickel a separate HV centrifuge on the gold feeder. A second LV machine could separate nickel but would spend longer on the ore required for 128 Nichrome. HV costs four advanced circuits and two HV motors. There are eight Nanoprocessors already in ME. EV would cost four extreme circuits and two EV motors. Use the native HV processing pattern, return the platinum by-product, and measure a full nickel order reaching ME. The new machine is still a build order.

The personal grid still handles motors and bus components. Page 53's motor stock advice applies across voltage tiers. The network has started eight MV motors in one order. Give HV motors and conveyors a shelf at the Molecular Assembler and encode the four-wire copper subassembly as well. Page 104 explains why the chosen circuit in the pattern matters when ME has to make it. The IV Quantumprocessor recipe gives four extreme circuits per board with 72 mB solder, but it needs the IV assembler. Keep tungstensteel and the Nichrome furnace moving beside the QBit work. Page 265's 50 mB radon recipe uses the same indium line needed later.

For the next review, measure oxygen and acid trips, a complete nickel delivery, and CPU contention after these orders become available. The old workshop's cargo conduit already occupies the east face of the EV indium reactor. The west and north spaces are open. Keep those available for a dedicated 150 EU/t leaching machine feeding the 600 EU/t reduction stage when the next indium batch is due.
