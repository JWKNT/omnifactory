# Routing several chemical fluids together

Quest 57 describes Ender Fluid Conduits. They can carry several fluids in the same route and have color channels and filters. Its reward is one nickel. Our PVC runs needed separate player trips for vinyl chloride, oxygen, hydrochloric acid and ethanol. The first compact build worth measuring is the oxygen drum feeding the two north polymer reactors. Those reactors both use oxygen continuously when organic feed is present. They can share one supply color without a changeover.

JEI gives eight pressurized conduits from six Conduit Binder and three glass in the 16 EU/t assembler. The same inputs on the bench yield four. A second assembler operation upgrades one pressurized section, two single Vibrant Alloy Wires and six binder into eight Ender Fluid Conduits. The bench yields four. There are no fluid inputs for either assembler recipe. The two circuits for the freezer use 4 vibrant wires per array and 8 per mainframe. The wire order now adds the two for this first conduit batch to the same blast and wiremill run. There are 182 pearls, 27 gold and 37 glowstone dust in the last survey. Two vibrant wires already existed in ME.

Keep the freezer machines working during this build. Confirm the route length before placing the pipe; several segments can share the existing RF and ME conduit blocks. Test with one fluid and both reactor inlets. Add ethanol and diluted acid on separate channels once the eastern bay is in place. The new pipes will still need extract and insert settings at each end.

Both assembler recipes delivered eight. Quest 57 paid its omninickel. All eight Ender Fluid Conduits are installed. From the drum the line runs through (6,83,0), turns at z=-1, drops to y=80 and runs under the PVC reactor at (6,80,-4). Its inlet accepts insertion, and extraction from the reactor is set to NEVER. After enabling extraction at the drum the reactor rose from 7,000 to 14,000 mB in one second, then filled to 64,000 mB. The drum still held 123,000 mB. The portable loader for PVC is retired. A second upgrade order uses one of seven spare pressurized sections. Two segments will reach polyethylene under the acid recovery still. Close unwanted fluid ports before reopening the drum. Six advanced sections will remain for other fluids.

The second assembler batch delivered eight Ender Fluid Conduits. Two extend the floor line under the still at (6,80,-5) to the polyethylene reactor at (6,80,-6). I switched off extraction at the source, disabled the adjacent diesel drum port and the upward port into the still, then reopened extraction. The Yeta Wrench cycles the mode when right-clicking the visible pipe arm. From (8,80,-5) this closed the still connection even though its connector screen was inaccessible. The PE reactor rose from empty to 4,200 mB in the first measured second. Both polymer reactors now draw directly from ruby oxygen. Six advanced conduit sections remain.

The same trunk now carries polyethylene back from the north reactor. Two new Ender Fluid Conduits at (7,83,0) and (7,83,1) reach the top of the MV electronics assembler. Its insertion color is blue. The oxygen inlet under the reactor was hard to reach for configuration, so two more sections at (6,80,-7) and (6,81,-7) give the reactor a serviceable north connection. Stand at (6,80,-8) to set its south-facing port to blue extraction, always active. The oxygen drum still extracts on green. There are two spare sections from this batch.

At commissioning the assembler rose from 44 to 1,556 mB, a delivery of all 1,512 mB available. Afterward it processed three resistor cycles and one transistor cycle. The portable polyethylene transfer to this assembler has been retired. A stock order now asks for more ethylene when the assembler falls below 1,152 mB, enough for four transistor cycles. The oxygen supply and the last leg of polyethylene delivery run in-game. Ethanol and acid transfers for the dehydration step still take player actions.
