# A light-fuel branch on the fifth furnace feeder

The workshop bought another 32 diamond ore for two quarters while its second ore lot was still being processed. The tier-three miner already needs a Turbo Diesel Generator. Use it for a measured power trial before the rest of that ship is ready. The live diesel-generator recipe accepts ordinary Light Fuel. At the recipe's reference voltage, 32 mB gives 305 ticks at 32 EU/t. A bucket therefore represents 305,000 EU before machine efficiency. Over a thousand buckets were in ME at the earlier refinery survey. The gas turbine would need a separate machine, and the Thermal Compression Dynamo is disabled in this instance.

The nine-conduit branch for the fifth furnace has a free space directly below the conduit at (36,83,-9). Put the four-amp HV CEU at (36,82,-9) with its FE output pointing up. Put the generator at (36,82,-8) with its EU output facing north into the converter. Both sit over the existing cobblestone floor with two blocks of headroom beneath them. There will be no EU cable connecting the generator to another voltage tier. The CEU takes an HV hull, four 4x Vibrant Alloy Wires, two Vibrant Energy Conduits, one chest and an Advanced circuit. The smaller converter is rated for 8,192 FE/t and can later accept additional HV generators. Leave eight single Vibrant Alloy Wires for the two miner field generators.

Start with one measured portable-tank charge and check both the generator's fuel consumption and the RF output reaching the feeder. Reserve the twelve buckets already marked Diesel for Draconium chemistry. Use only Light Fuel for this trial. Return the engine for the tier-three ship once its other parts have been funded. A second engine and a filtered fluid export can make the branch permanent if the first charge materially reduces diamond use.

The two HV pistons are ready. The engine and converter each need an HV hull, and the medium projector's HV output bus needs a third. There was one loose stainless plate at 104h02m. A hull uses an eight-plate casing and one more loose plate. The engine's two full gears take another eight stainless ingots. Five chrome dust, 858 manganese dust and 718 nickel dust are already in storage. Queue nine- or eighteen-ingot orders through the existing nine-dust alloy recipe and north blast furnace. A nine-dust lot uses six iron dust, one chrome, one manganese and one nickel. This assigns some incoming iron ore to stainless before other orders convert the entire lot to steel.

At 105h52m the generator and four-amp HV CEU were installed at the planned positions. The generator faces north and the CEU faces up. The conduit above it reports its only external connection downward. A 16,000 mB Light Fuel charge went through the generator's container input slot. Its tank read 15,488 mB after startup, then 14,976 mB with 7,168 EU in the generator and 15,886 EU in the converter. The shared grid also has two lapidary dynamos. Measure their use over the full diesel charge before choosing the number of permanent engines.

The next reading showed 13,952 mB of Light Fuel and 16,289 of 16,384 EU in the CEU. Its overhead conduit still reported a single external connection to the converter below. The grid was delivering 1,155 RF/t at the time of that reading, shared across all generators.

At 106h20m the trial had used 15,872 of its 16,000 mB, leaving less than the 512 mB needed for the next HV fuel cycle. ME still held 1,044 buckets of Light Fuel. Keep the current generator fueled while it waits for its ship, with 256 buckets protected for chemistry. A shared-grid reading cannot attribute individual diamonds saved to the generator. Once the ship takes this engine, build a replacement on the same CEU and run two ME glass cable segments from (35,82,−10) to a west-side filtered Light Fuel export bus. That gives the later diamond-block reserve a permanent additional power source.
