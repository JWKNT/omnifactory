# Polyethylene for the LuV hull and blank patterns

At 129h15m the MV assembler held 12 mB of polyethylene. The sheet caster held 96 mB, the press had produced four Lumium plates for the hull, and the workshop had a single sheet and no blank patterns. Five sheets, three fine silver wires and one eligible circuit make eight blank patterns. The hull assembler needs 288 mB in its own tank.

The dehydrator held 64 buckets of ethanol and only 50 mB of sulfuric acid. The neighboring chemical plant had three buckets of acid in its output. Refilling the dehydrator and temporarily directing ethylene to polyethylene restarted the line. The sheet tender can now draw a batch sized to the missing sheets. Once the LuV casing is ready it leaves 288 mB for the hull. The previous twenty-bucket transfer threshold was too high for this shared assembler.

At 129h29m the portable tank had moved 1,488 mB from the assembler to the caster. Together with the caster’s existing 96 mB that made eleven new sheets. ME stock rose from one to twelve. Reserve the next blanks for the north Assembly Line and the workshop processing machines.

At 131h35m an MV hull order had occupied a crafting CPU for over fourteen minutes. The assembler had its two copper cables and casing but only 108 of the required 288 mB polyethylene. The dehydrator had 64 buckets of ethanol and 650 mB sulfuric acid. The tender now treats an actual MV casing in the assembler as a reason to replenish polyethylene, even after the north converter is built. The dehydrator took acid and processed ethanol. The MV hull subsequently reached storage, its CPU was released, and 252 mB polyethylene remained in the assembler.
