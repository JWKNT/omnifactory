# 67-hour review

In two played hours the transfer log counted 91 player-loaded ingredient stacks and eleven ME deliveries verified by a stock increase. Nine of those deliveries were tungsten and two were tungstensteel. The silicone reactor took twelve ingredient stacks. Four trips moved 24 buckets of diluted hydrochloric acid. Nine stacks each went to the HV mill, centrifuge and electrolyzer. The IV assembler needs all these lines, but winding one more coil would do nothing for the withheld fuel or the blocked cable orders.

The existing three crafting CPUs were all busy in 99 of 574 ten-second samples. All were idle in 123. I was wrong to treat either number as a reason to postpone the quartz supply. The missing quartz held up a fuel ingredient even while glass and sand were available. A 64-glass batch went into the LV cell after I removed a rule that required 16 pearls before it would crush glass. Its ME interface is now in place. Test a second delivery during a tungsten order. Keep the north sand generator and the new water-fed MV clay pair producing at the same time. Put the sand and clay behind the shared drawer controller when the bus is ready. Page 97 pays a quarter and explains why a stock request needs a bounded inventory.

An Enderman pristine pays six pearls. One pearl and one quartz make a clathrate, whose dust and one clay block make four polymer. I installed a brown polymer inlet at the rotating chamber using the same conduit that already extracts its pristine matter. The Shulker has its own inlet, and 41 diamonds were waiting in the two dynamos after its recovery. The Enderman should take fuel while pearls are low and release it to the other chambers once there are 48. Check the counter plus its queued clay for an unattended increase. The long-term goal is to have both models running when the player is away. Copper already has a dedicated Spider chamber and a cutoff. If iron remains short when pearls are stocked, the Zombie should get its own chamber before another ore purchase.

All four fluid cells hit their five-type limit. There was plenty of space for existing fluids. Moving thirteen buckets of chlorine to the titanium reactor and one bucket of allyl chloride to the board reactor restored useful work. The second drive is back in the IV build queue. The old LV distillery had a bucket of diluted sulfuric acid occupying its only inlet, preventing it from processing the eighteen buckets of diluted hydrochloric acid in ME. For fresh acid, the existing MV desulfurizer has permanent hydrogen input, an unfiltered fluid return and an idle reaction slot. Chlorine and hydrogen take 60 base ticks at 8 EU/t per bucket. Compare that with 1,200 ticks in an LV still or 600 overclocked ticks in MV for a bucket recovered from two diluted buckets. Use the desulfurizer for the shortage, recover the by-product when the still is free, and measure the next rubber batch. In a well-stocked run the silicone tender can now use four sulfur charges together.

Build a separate 4k CPU above the north cable at (9,84,-6). A fourth 1k would be cheaper. Both use the same crafting unit, and the 4k component needs two additional 1k components, one Logic Processor and one Good-grade circuit beyond the first component, plus glass. Page 118 registers the component before assembly and pays a nickel. Use an HV-batched Microcircuit or a stocked Refined Processor for the Good slot. Page 104 explains why an autocrafting pattern should explicitly name the economy circuit. The first interface went to quartz; the next serves the HV grinder. Page 140 also explains why a second inscriber for Printed Silicon needs an actual output extractor. Keep the two dark steel blocks reserved for the drive. Check independently advancing tungsten, quartz, and interface orders before counting the fourth CPU as a throughput gain.

The 48-dust bauxite order and a small smelt from the second ore variant supplied two complete separator charges. I measured 32 aluminium dust, then 27 aluminium ingots in ME. Four Printed Silicon became four Logic Processors, then four of each interface core. The MV nickel washer took 36 crushed ore, and the Kanthal furnace accepted ten stainless dust.
