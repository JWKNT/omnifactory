Stainless steel for the first HV hull and mainframe

Quest 93 checks one HV Machine Hull after stainless steel. The bench version takes eight stainless plates for the casing and a ninth for the hull, two polyethylene sheets and two insulated single gold cables. Assembling the casing with two cables and 288 mB polyethylene saves that ninth plate. The planned first stainless charge yields eight ingots plus a dust sample. Two of those ingots are already committed to the first Refined Processor Array.

Budget two more nine-dust mixes before building the hull. They take twelve iron dust, two nickel, two chrome and two manganese. Six of the remaining ruby ore yield twelve dust after milling, hammering and washing, enough for two chrome. For manganese, two nickels buy 32 pyrolusite ore and an ordinary furnace gives a manganese ingot per ore. Mill the two ingots needed. The single manganese ingot in storage belongs to the first mix. The later mainframe also needs four stainless frames; three plates, five rods and a wrench make all four. The lathe returns two rods per ingot.

Gold cable insulation requires an assembler. A single gold wire, circuit 24 and 144 mB rubber make one cable. Silicone rubber and styrene-butadiene rubber are alternatives at 36 and 72 mB per cable. The bench splitting recipes require insulated cable as their input. Use the wiremill for the gold wires and the shared rubber extractor once the ME Conduit run is complete. Keep gold for the vibrant alloy wire in refined circuits. These are the bills for the next tier; the new hull and mainframe have yet to run.
