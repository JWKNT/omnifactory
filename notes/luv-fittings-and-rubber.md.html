Fittings and rubber for the first LuV assembler

Eight of the second mainframe's nine buses were stocked at 93h13m, and the first of the two missing arrays subsequently returned to ME. While the circuits run, prepare the fittings and chemistry for ten LuV motors, two pistons, two arms and two conveyors.

Each piston needs 32 rounds, six plates, four rings, four short rods, four single YBCO cables, two small gears, one motor and one full gear. The last bus holds circuit 2. For a conveyor the order is 32 rounds, four rings, two motors, two plates and two cables, with circuit 1 in the sixth bus. Its liquid is 1,440 mB styrene-butadiene rubber plus 250 mB lubricant. Use the south fluid inlet at (25,82,−18) for rubber. The circuit is reusable. Remove it between different bills. The arm's live JEI order is six cables, six Extreme circuits, four long rods, three small gears, two Elite circuits, two Master circuits, two motors, one full gear and one piston. The ZenScript text puts both higher circuit grades earlier. Use the live slot order and verify the first cycle before repeating.

The four rings from one HSS-G ingot cost 192 EU/t. A full gear takes four ingots at 256 EU/t. The small gear costs one ingot. A common 512 V extruder can run all three dies in batches. Its planned site is (17,81,−6), on the south side of the foil mill's double gold feeder. The work order for the HV piston has started. Keep two ingots free for the motor rods before feeding fittings. Both grid hammering and extrusion work for rings; extrusion yields more rings per ingot. The full gear also has a Compactor recipe at the same four-ingot cost. The extruder serves the rings on the same power branch.

The two conveyor charges need 2,880 mB rubber. Three nine-dust cures with one sulfur each produce 3,888 mB. The oxygen polymerization recipe pays three dust per cycle. Nine cycles require 972 mB butadiene, 324 mB styrene and 18,000 mB oxygen. The air version pays only one dust. One bucket each of ethylene and benzene makes a bucket of styrene and two of hydrogen. At 93h17m ME held 3,200 mB ethylene, 96 buckets of heavy fuel, 988 buckets of light fuel and over 2.6 million mB oxygen. No rubber, butadiene, styrene or benzene was in fluid storage. Steam-cracking 20 buckets of light fuel takes 40 buckets of steam. Distillery program 5 returns one bucket of butadiene from that charge. Program 3 can supply benzene from a separate seven buckets of steam-cracked light fuel. The existing crude tower can instead recover both together if its outputs are available. A second distillation tower would keep those by-products available without interrupting crude or borrowing the propene still.
