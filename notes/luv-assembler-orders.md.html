What the first LuV assembler actually costs

Page 283 pays one nickel. Its grid recipe requires two ultimate circuits, two LuV arms, two LuV conveyors, a LuV hull and two single niobium-titanium cables. The first Quantum Processor Mainframe opens page 531 and its two-quarter reward. Plan on a second mainframe for the machine. Pages 283 and 155 then lead to the first Crystal Circuit, which pays one dollar.

The two arms each take two LuV motors and one LuV piston. Each piston takes another motor. Both conveyors also take two motors apiece. That makes ten LuV motors before the assembler can be built. Each motor consumes four stacks of fine annealed copper wire, two long HSS-G rods, one long magnetic neodymium rod, two yttrium barium cuprate cables, 144 mB solder and 250 mB lubricant. The first ten motors alone need 2,560 fine wires. Keep the native west annealing furnace, stage a wiremill pattern, and allow room for ten concurrent winding lots.

The existing north transformer supplies 8,192 EU/t, enough for the 8,000 EU/t mainframe. The LuV motor needs 10,240 EU/t for 300 ticks, the conveyor and piston each need 15,360 EU/t for 600 ticks, and the arm needs 20,480 EU/t for 600 ticks. The LuV hatch can stay. Upgrade its supply before committing the first motor load. One arm costs 12,288,000 EU before any losses. The mainframe's five-million-EU buffer needs expansion or sustained higher generation for this stage. The automatic item-relay plan is in assembly-line-item-relays.md. Reserve one of the four fluid hatches for lubricant and another for styrene-butadiene rubber.

At LuV the initial Crystal Circuit recipe takes eight SMD resistors, four SMD transistors, two fine niobium-titanium wires, a multilayer board, a Crystal CPU and a Nano CPU, at 10,000 EU/t. Choose 72 mB solder instead of 144 mB tin when solder stock allows. JEI also lists a Crystal SoC route at 86,000 EU/t, beyond the first machine.

The first two mainframes should run with a single IV hatch. See first-quantum-mainframe.md for the measured overclock calculation. Afterward, keep that 8,192 EU/t feed and put the saved LuV hatch in another roof casing. A separate four-amp IV CEF can deliver two 8,192 V amps to the second hatch, for a combined 24,576 EU/t. This exceeds the 20,480 EU/t arm load. Both hatches together still put the controller in the LuV overclock tier, where the motor, conveyor, piston and arm recipes run at their base rates. Check the calculation on the actual controller for each recipe.

The IV CEF recipe uses one elite circuit, an IV hull, eight Lumium wires folded into two fourfold wires, a chest and four Lumium Energy Conduits. The assembler makes all four conduits from six binder, two more Lumium wires and one End Steel Energy Conduit. The grid makes three. The other supply option takes a second IV hull, four tungsten cables, one vanadium-gallium cable and two Power ICs for a LuV step-up transformer. The second hatch already in stock saves those parts. Use a fresh buffer at the second converter and measure stored energy on each branch before an arm run. Four basic Flux Capacitors reserve 10 million EU at the new converter. The old converter can contribute about 4.9 million EU during 600 ticks at full output. Confirm how the installed controller draws on the two hatches during a motor before committing the longer loads.

All sixteen LuV motion crafts

The ten motors, two pistons, two conveyors and two arms together need 44 single Yttrium Barium Cuprate cables. At two wires per ingot that is 22 ingots. Nine cold ingots remain from the breeder campaign, so one 13-dust mixer charge covers the difference exactly. The other ingredients for that charge are one yttrium dust, two barium, three copper and 7,000 mB oxygen. There are 20 barite ore and 96 tiny piles of Rare Earth on hand. The blast recipe heats one dust in 4,590 ticks at 120 EU/t before overclocking. Cool each hot ingot in the freezer for 281 ticks at 120 EU/t. Reachable routes and the first mixer run are in barium-for-superconductors.md. The MV wiremill tender can stage the first 18 wires from existing ingots when the tungsten wire reserve allows. Wrapping all 44 takes 3,168 mB silicone rubber or 6,336 mB styrene-butadiene rubber.

Across all sixteen crafts, budget 4,000 mB lubricant, 2,880 mB solder and 2,880 mB styrene-butadiene rubber for the conveyors. The component bus bill includes 28 long HSS-G rods, 128 HSS-G rounds, 16 plates, 16 rings, eight ordinary rods, ten small gears and four full gears. Preserve the four HSS-G frames for the first mainframe and allow eight frames for the pair. The two arms take twelve extreme circuits, four elite circuits and four master circuits in total. The displayed Crystal Circuit for the elite slot and Energy Flow Circuit for the master slot are ore-dictionary examples. Use the existing basic Quantumprocessor, upgraded Quantum Processor and Quantum Processor Array production for these three grades before the LuV assembler exists. Both mainframes have their own array requirements in addition to the four arrays in the arms.

At 91h18m the fourth casing order is running and a second IV motor has been ordered. The copper shelf has 331 copper ingots, 65 annealed ingots and 34 fine annealed wires. Start accumulating the first LuV motor's 256 fine wires during the foil queue. On the MV component wiremill each annealed ingot yields two single wires and each single yields four fine wires. Reserve 48 annealed ingots for the IV winding bill and allow at most 64 fine wires per order while at least two CPUs are free. Stop at the first motor's four stacks until the casing order and north power upgrade advance.
