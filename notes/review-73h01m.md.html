Factory review at 73 hours

Since the 71-hour review the transfer log counted 102 player-loaded stacks. Ten container trips moved 7,200 mB of epoxy resin. Five trips moved 20,000 mB of chlorine, and four moved 64,000 mB oxygen. The coater, ore mill and electric furnace each took eight ingredient stacks. Of 282 ten-second CPU samples, all four units were busy in 46 and all four were idle in 58. The item drive had four free item types across six cells.

Keep the working LV board coater through the first IV circuit batches. The chemical recipe takes 500 ticks at 10 EU/t for one board. With continuous feed that is 144 boards per hour. The MV version halves the cycle at four times the power draw and needs a bronze rotor, an MV motor and hull, two circuits, two copper cables and two glass. An HV replacement needs a steel rotor, HV motor and hull, and gold cables. An EV replacement takes an EV hull and two circuits in the same grade we need for new machines. The caster and coater already transfer sheets and boards without a player. Their interruptions came from missing resin and copper foil. Finish the foil arm and its filtered interface before adding more coating machines. The MV extruder made sixteen steel bolts from two ingots in two 15-tick cycles; the molecular shelf can turn those into thirty-two small coils with fine wire. Keep that machine stocked with steel between shape changes.

Give the next storage housing to the second drive above the pattern aisle. An additional 1k item cell provides 63 types, and a fluid cell provides five. This matters more immediately than another crafting CPU. The second drive preserves the ten installed cells and puts the new ones on an existing cable. Page 730 explains why item-only processing patterns still need a dedicated fluid supply. Keep the IV assembler on solder and place its interface on the north face. The existing four-amp EV feeder can serve the planned step-up; verify its output in game before putting the new assembler under load.

The cheaper circuit route changes again at IV. The two robot arms can use different elite circuits. The first used a Microprocessor Mainframe; the other can use a Nano Array from two Lumium ingots. Once the IV assembler is paid, stop ordering extra Nano Mainframes for its bill. The first four Quantumprocessors need one multi-layer board and one QBit CPU; reserve three outputs for the upgraded processor on page 268. Both pages pay two quarters. For the titanium casing I placed one order for seven plates. The HV reactor, north furnace, magnesium chloride recovery, freezer and compressor delivered all seven while the fourth furnace made Lumium. Use the same network route for later hulls.

For the next refinery change, reserve an accessible outlet at the resin reactor and independently controlled feeds to the fiber bath and sheet caster. The north conduit already carries air; its resin connection is disabled. Ten resin moves in two hours justify a separate route. Before installing it, check that its ports still allow service from the aisles at (10,80,-13) and (13,80,-11). In the meantime give ordinary sheets priority while titanium frames can fund the older elite circuit, and give the fiber bath priority for the Nano and quantum lines.
