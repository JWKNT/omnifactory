Third blast furnace and coil supply

The 36 stainless dust for the EV bay run at 120 EU/t for 600 ticks each in the west furnace. That is eighteen minutes for one material before the next aluminium, energetic alloy or sterling silver charge can start. Meanwhile the north furnace is tied into a productive titanium loop. Page 580, Blast Furnaces Galore, recommends retaining working furnaces for passive stocks and building higher-temperature furnaces alongside them. Three spare controllers already sit in the parts chest from page 541.

Plan a third structure east of the EV power line. Keep the existing furnace and the coolant return available to the other lines. The first new floor spans x16–24 and z−8–0. The proposed furnace footprint x21–23, z−6 through −4 leaves both a west service aisle and room to extend the deck east. Survey its power and hot-item connections before placing the shell.

Price the new machine with Nichrome coils. They reach 3,600 K and count toward page 159, which pays a quarter for the full set. The sixteen coils use 128 ingots in all. Twenty cooled Nichrome are already in ME. Twenty-two five-ingot batches in the existing Kanthal furnace make the outstanding 108 plus two spare. That takes 88 nickel ingots and 22 chrome ingots. JEI gives 800 ticks per five hot ingots before freezing. Additional electrical capacity and feeding the mats will decide actual throughput. Check the quarter for the full set before installing any coils.

The first nickel microminer mission returns 64 ore. Until that is available, two nickels buy 32 nickel ore. Ruby costs four nickels for 32 ore. The verified wash, second grind, centrifuge and electrolyzer route returned eighteen full chrome from eighteen ruby ore, with aluminium as a by-product. Twenty-four purified ruby from previous washes are still in ME. Run them first and stage new ores for the HV grinder, where the extra output slots can capture by-products. Budget the four-amp HV and EV branches separately, and leave floor space for eight complete blast furnaces in the longer-term layout.
