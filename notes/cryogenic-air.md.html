Sizing the air line for doped silicon

Kanthal opens the temperature requirement for glowstone-doped boules. The rest of the production line is larger than the furnace recipe suggests. JEI asks for 64 silicon dust, 8 glowstone and 8 buckets of argon per boule, at 480 EU/t for 3,000 ticks. The latest workshop survey held 63 silicon dust, 19 glowstone, 17 CPU chips and three plain wafers.

Quest 174 accepts an LV, MV, HV or EV air collector and pays a nickel. Leave its top unobstructed and put its output against a tank. The freezer converts four buckets of air to four of liquid air in 400 ticks at base voltage. A tower takes 53 buckets of liquid air and returns 40 nitrogen, 11 oxygen, one carbon dioxide and one noble gases. The next tower recipe needs 53 buckets of noble gases for 35 argon, 11 neon, five helium, one krypton and one xenon. That first argon batch therefore requires 2,809 buckets of liquid air. It buys four boules with three buckets of argon left.

A single freezer doing only this at base speed takes nearly four hours to supply that first batch. Keep hot titanium cooling on its direct item line. Price a separate freezer or higher voltage for air before committing the existing freezer to a continuous fluid supply. Reserve floor for both towers with separate product tanks. Page 171 recommends two towers and pays a dollar. Page 175 pays a quarter for a bucket of argon and explicitly recommends running the line continuously. Tower recipes draw 500 and 750 EU/t respectively, so budget the feeder for their overlap. A full freezer output or a full nitrogen tank can stop argon upstream. Measure liquid air produced per minute and prove each tank's exit before expanding collector count.
