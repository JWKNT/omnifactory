Each coil takes eight double wires. The wiremill and wire extruder both give two single wires per ingot. Cutting a pressed plate gives only one. Combining singles in the grid and winding them around a reusable wrench needs 128 ingots for sixteen coils. The bundler and assembler are alternatives when their programmed patterns are in place.

The mixer and grid both yield nine HSS-G dust from five tungstensteel, two molybdenum, one chrome and one vanadium dust. Fourteen batches would supply 126 fresh ingots. Together with the three left from the frame order that covers the coil set with one spare. Reserve 70 tungstensteel, 28 molybdenum, 14 chrome and 14 vanadium for that order, separately from the Assembly Line hardware. The east furnace makes two tungstensteel per tungsten and steel pair. At the fourth furnace's measured 1,125 ticks per HSS-G, 126 ingots take about 118 minutes before cooling. Start in batches after the tungstensteel supply catches up. At 94h25m the remaining 26 molybdenite ore warranted a different route from the first two frames. One ore in the HV mill yields six crushed ore. Mill those again into six impure dust, with a chance for extra molybdenum at both stages. Centrifuging all six returns six molybdenite dust and eighteen tiny molybdenum dust. Electrolyzing the six clean dust gives two molybdenum dust and four sulfur dust. Nine tiny dust pack to one full dust. That makes four guaranteed molybdenum per ore, plus the mill by-products, compared with one ingot from direct smelting. Start with four ores for sixteen guaranteed molybdenum and sixteen sulfur, then compare the returns. Sulfur is also needed for the chemical line.
