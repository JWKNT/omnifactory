Keeping the ore hammer available

The shared macerator doubles raw iron and copper into crushed ore. In our current route the bench hammer turns the crushed ore into impure dust for smelting. When the hammer wore out, 25 crushed iron and eight crushed copper were in the Drive. The compressor and alloy smelter were spending the few incoming iron ingots before the bench could replace it.

The order planner now holds back six ingots when a job needs a replacement hammer. The iron hammer recipe is six ingots and one stick, JEI bench index 5. The sample hammer shown as an ingredient on crushed ore recipes is made of Darmstadtium. The iron hammer fits the same tool slot. The electric furnace accepted eight raw iron ore for the replacement while the shared macerator continued doubling copper. The other two ingots can make a mortar with five stone. The clay line can use that mortar on leftover sand or gravel before taking more cobblestone.

That eight-ore charge made both tools. The new mortar immediately turned the five sand left by the previous batch into five Dust Blocks. The water-fed reactor accepted all five. For the next hammer, the workshop will use six diamonds and a stick if at least 54 diamonds are stocked. GregTech reports base tool durability of 1,280 for diamond, 368 for emerald and 256 for iron. Six diamonds leave the 48-diamond operating reserve intact. JEI bench index 109 is the direct diamond recipe.
