The circuit after the first Crystal Circuit

Page 155 opens the Crystal Processor on page 292, which pays five coins and opens the Crystal Processor Array branch. Four-output Crystal Circuits already have a processing card in the north LuV assembler. Keep the first 7×7 table and its ore and gem flights ahead of this job. They supply the osmium and engraved crystal chips the processor chain depends on.

The first Crystal Processor takes three Crystal Circuits, one separate Crystal CPU, one QBit CPU, one multilayer fiber-reinforced board, six fine niobium-titanium wires and four each of small coil, SMD capacitor and SMD diode. JEI lists 144 mB tin or 72 mB solder in the same assembler. Either takes 100 ticks at 20,000 EU/t for one processor. Use the existing solder export. Allow six more fine wires beyond the eight assigned to the first sixteen Crystal Circuits. Keep one engraved CPU for this upgrade. The four-amp IV CEF can deliver three IV packets in a tick in principle. Verify at least two million EU banked and keep the Assembly Line idle for the first test. A completed processor in ME and a claimed page are the test.

The next page, 534, moves back to the Assembly Line. The recipe displayed here uses four Energy Flow Circuits, four Enderium wires, two Draconium plates, one HPIC, one improved Ender Eye, one multilayer board and eight each of SMD transistor and diode. It consumes 720 mB solder at 30,000 EU/t for 300 ticks. Page 534 pays five coins. Inspect the ingredient alternatives on the actual line when it becomes available and verify both the input voltage and stored energy before dispatch. The current two-hatch mainframe trial ran at only 8,000 EU/t.
