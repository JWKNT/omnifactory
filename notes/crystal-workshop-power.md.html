Power for the Crystal Circuit workshop

JEI gives the first four Crystal Circuits at 10,000 EU/t for 200 ticks. Crystal CPU engraving takes 7,600 EU/t. Their machines need LuV and IV respectively. In this instance, GregTech's RecipeLogicEnergy checks the machine's rated input voltage. EnergyContainerHandler accepts smaller packets. Two IV packets per tick can cover the first crystal circuit in a LuV assembler. Test this with a charge before committing larger lots.

A new LuV CEF would take four Signalum Energy Conduits, eight single Signalum wires in two bundles, another LuV hull and a Master circuit. The north workshop already has a four-amp IV CEF and two hardened capacitors. Once the first LuV assembler has been crafted, extend that CEF to both the line hatch and the crystal workshop. Keep the current direct feed while producing the LuV motion parts.

The roof blocks at (22,86,−18) and (22,86,−19) were both air at 96h36m. Fit two 2x Tungsten Cables there, above the CEF and its LuV input hatch. The in-game tooltip gives 8,192 V, four amps and a loss of two EU per block. A single tungsten cable is rated for only two amps. With the line idle, turn the hatch front up toward its new cable, then turn the CEF output up. Verify both cable connections, the formed controller and a replenishing hatch. The original IV input on the next slice stays powered by its own transformer. If the overhead connection fails its test, the original south-facing hatch and north-facing CEF can be restored with the wrench.

The space immediately east of the first cable at (23,86,−18) is clear for the LuV assembler. The west side at (21,86,−18) is clear for the IV engraver. The first recipe alone consumes two million EU. Start the workshop's first charged test with the Assembly Line idle. Track both machine charge and actual recipe progress before treating the shared supply as sufficient. Give the assembler a solder inlet and ME item interface, and leave overhead access for both. The 7×7 table and crystal materials can advance independently.
